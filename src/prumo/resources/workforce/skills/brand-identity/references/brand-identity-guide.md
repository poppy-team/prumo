# Brand Identity Reference Guide

## 1. Brand Architecture Models
Understanding how brands within a portfolio relate to one another:
- **Monolithic (Branded House):** A single master brand is used across all products. (e.g., Google, Virgin). *Benefit: High brand equity transfer.*
- **Endorsed (Sub-brands):** Products have their own identities but are explicitly linked to a parent brand. (e.g., Marriott, Apple iPad). *Benefit: Flexibility with parent reassurance.*
- **Pluralistic (House of Brands):** A portfolio of distinct, independent brands with no visible connection to the parent. (e.g., Procter & Gamble). *Benefit: High market segmentation, isolated risk.*

## 2. Responsive Logo Design
Logos must adapt to different contexts while retaining brand recognition:
- **Hero (Full):** The complete logo lockup (Symbol + Wordmark + Tagline). Used on desktop sites, large print, billboards.
- **Compact (Stacked):** Symbol above wordmark. Used for square formats, social media profiles, vertical banners.
- **Micro (Icon/Monogram):** The symbol or first letter alone. Used for favicons, app icons, smartwatches.

## 3. Logo Design Principles
- **Scalability:** Must be legible at 16px and 1600px.
- **Reproducibility:** Must work in full color, single color (monochrome), and inverted (white on black).
- **Distinctiveness:** Unique shape and silhouette.
- **Relevance:** Appropriate for the target industry and audience.

## 4. Color Theory & Accessibility
- Utilize **OKLCH** for perceptually uniform color palettes, allowing for mathematically consistent lightness and chroma steps.
- Ensure text over brand colors meets **WCAG 2.1 AA** standards (contrast ratio $\ge 4.5:1$).
