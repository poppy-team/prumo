# Advertising Design Reference Guide

## 1. The AIDA Model in Design
The AIDA model outlines the cognitive stages a consumer goes through. Visually, this translates to:
*   **Attention:** Achieved through high contrast, disruption of patterns, and dominant scale.
*   **Interest:** Sustained through rhythmic typography, subheadings, and easily scannable "F" or "Z" reading patterns.
*   **Desire:** Fostered by high-quality lifestyle photography, color psychology (e.g., warm colors for excitement, cool for trust), and visual social proof (e.g., star ratings).
*   **Action:** Driven by isolation (whitespace), directional cues (arrows, line of sight), and stark contrast.

## 2. Visual Persuasion Triad (Aristotle's Rhetoric)
*   **Ethos (Credibility):** Clean layouts, adherence to brand standards, high-resolution imagery, trust badges (e.g., "Secure Checkout").
*   **Pathos (Emotion):** Imagery showing human faces (especially expressing the desired end-state emotion), color palettes mapped to emotional responses.
*   **Logos (Logic):** Clear data visualization (charts, numbers), bullet points for features, logical grouping (Gestalt principles).

## 3. Call to Action (CTA) Optimization
*   **The "Squint Test":** If you squint at the design, the CTA should be the clearest, most prominent colored blob on the page.
*   **Contrast Formula:** Utilize the Relative Luminance formula to ensure the CTA text has a ratio of $\ge 4.5:1$ against the button background, and the button itself contrasts sharply with the surrounding canvas.
*   **Size:** Minimum touch target size of 44x44 CSS pixels (Apple/Google accessibility standards).

## 4. A/B Testing Fundamentals
*   **Single Variable Change:** Never change more than one element (e.g., change the headline OR the hero image, not both) to ensure you know *why* performance changed.
*   **Statistical Significance:** A test is only valid when it reaches statistical significance (typically 95% confidence level), requiring adequate sample size.
