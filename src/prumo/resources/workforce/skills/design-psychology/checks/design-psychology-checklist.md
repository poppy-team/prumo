# Design Psychology Audit Checklist

## Gestalt Principles
- [ ] **Proximity:** Are related elements grouped closely together?
- [ ] **Similarity:** Do elements with similar functions share similar visual attributes (color, shape, size)?
- [ ] **Closure:** Are incomplete shapes or loading states easily recognizable by the user's brain?
- [ ] **Figure-Ground:** Is there clear contrast between foreground elements (text/actions) and the background?
- [ ] **Prägnanz:** Is the interface as simple and symmetrical as possible?

## Decision-Making & Interaction
- [ ] **Hick's Law:** Are complex menus broken down using progressive disclosure?
- [ ] **Hick's Law:** Is the number of choices ($n$) minimized for critical decision paths?
- [ ] **Fitts's Law:** Are primary Calls to Action (CTAs) large enough?
- [ ] **Fitts's Law:** Are touch targets at least $44 \times 44$px?
- [ ] **Fitts's Law:** Are edge/corner targets utilized effectively for quick access?

## Memory & Cognitive Load
- [ ] **Miller's Law:** Is content chunked into groups of 5-9 items?
- [ ] **Extraneous Load:** Have decorative elements that do not convey information been removed or subdued?
- [ ] **Intrinsic Load:** Is the core task broken down into understandable steps?
- [ ] **Serial Position Effect:** Are the most important items placed at the beginning (primacy) or end (recency) of lists/menus?

## Perception & Behavioral Patterns
- [ ] **Von Restorff Effect:** Does the primary CTA visually stand out from surrounding elements?
- [ ] **Jakob's Law:** Does the design use familiar, conventional patterns for common actions (e.g., shopping cart icon, profile top-right)?
- [ ] **Doherty Threshold:** Are system responses visually indicated within 400ms (e.g., skeleton screens, spinners, optimistic UI)?
- [ ] **Peak-End Rule:** Is the final step of the user journey satisfying, clear, and reassuring?
